Same merchant. Same amount. Two lines on the statement, a day or two apart. The Federal Trade Commission's page on card disputes opens with exactly this: "Sometimes a company may bill you twice for the same thing."

Here's how to sort it out without losing track.

## The short answer
Confirm it's really a double charge. Ask the merchant to reverse one. If they don't, dispute it with your card company in writing, and do it fast. For consumer credit cards, federal rules give you **60 days** from the first statement that showed the error.

## Step by step
1. **Make sure it's really twice.** Line up both charges: date, amount, and the exact merchant text. Check your receipts or invoices. Did you buy twice? Two deliveries, two services, two orders? If not, keep going.
2. **Check that both have posted.** If one is still marked pending, ask your card company what that means for your account before you file anything.
3. **Call the merchant first.** The merchant may be able to reverse it directly. Write down the date, who you spoke with, and what they promised.
4. **Call your card company if the merchant doesn't fix it.** The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau says to call the card company "right away."
5. **Follow up in writing.** Don't skip this step. The CFPB says that to protect your rights, you must also send a **written** billing error notice within **60 calendar days** after the charge appeared on your statement. The FTC says to send it to the address for billing inquiries, not the one where you mail payments. Include your name, address, account number and a description of the mistake. Certified mail with a return receipt gives you proof.
6. **Keep a file.** Copies of your letter, receipts and notes on every call. The CFPB suggests writing down the dates of follow-up calls and keeping it all together.
7. **Know the clock on their side.** Under the same rules, the card company must acknowledge your dispute in writing within 30 days of getting it (unless it's already resolved), and resolve it within 90 days.

## While you wait
The FTC says you can hold off paying the disputed amount and related finance charges while the card company investigates. You're still expected to pay the rest of the bill. If you already paid the charge, the CFPB says you can still dispute it, but you probably won't see the money until the card company decides you were right.

## Three honest caveats
- **Business cards may be different.** The federal billing-error rules are part of 12 CFR part 1026, and that same part exempts credit "primarily for a business, commercial or agricultural purpose." So the 60-day process above is written for consumer accounts. Your business card agreement may offer its own dispute process. Read it, or call the number on the back of the card.
- **Don't wait on the merchant forever.** Calling them first is sensible, but the 60-day clock keeps running while you do.
- **This isn't legal advice.** If a charge turns into a real fight, talk to your card company or a professional.

## Verdict
Check your last statement today. If you see a double, start the clock on your side: merchant call, then a written notice. Regret meter if you skip it: high, because the window closes.
