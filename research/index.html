How do I spot a duplicate invoice before I pay it?

What we checked
FTC guidance for small businesses and payables guidance from two universities.
The result
At Indiana University, the biggest single cause of potential duplicates was the same invoice sent in by both the supplier and staff: more than 27% of them (article dated November 2024). The FTC warns that some fake invoices arrive marked "past due" to add urgency.

If this is you, do this: If a bill lands twice or says "past due," check vendor, invoice number, invoice date and amount against what you've already paid before you pay it.

Why was my business charged twice, and how do I get it back?

What we checked
FTC and CFPB guidance and the federal billing-error rule (12 CFR 1026.13).
The result
For consumer credit cards, a written billing error notice is due within 60 days of the first statement that showed the error. The same federal rules exempt credit used mainly for business, so a business card may follow its own dispute terms.

If this is you, do this: If you see the same charge twice, call the merchant first, then send your card company a written notice. Don't let the clock run.

How do I find subscriptions I forgot I'm paying for?

What we checked
FTC guidance on free trials and auto-renewals, and the Federal Register record of the FTC's subscription rule.
The result
One month of statements hides quarterly and yearly charges, so we start with three. The FTC notes a renewal can charge more than last time, especially after a promotional rate ends. A one-click cancel rule isn't something to count on.

If this is you, do this: If you can't say who uses a recurring charge in ten seconds, put it on the cancel list and keep proof when you cancel.

What we checked
Five checks you can run yourself with statements you already have.

Do Connecticut's 2026 privacy changes reach small businesses?

What we checked
The Connecticut Attorney General's FAQ, its summary of the July 2026 changes, and its September 2026 release.
The result
The consumer headcount test dropped from 100,000 to 35,000. Handling sensitive data, outside of just completing payments, can bring a business under the law no matter how many customers it has. This is a summary, not legal advice.

If this is you, do this: If you collect account numbers with a password or security code, or Social Security numbers, read the AG's three tests and ask a Connecticut lawyer.

How do you know if a small AI model is good enough to trust?

What we checked
Small AI models run on our own computer after hours, using made-up data only.
The result
A test that looked like a clear pass failed when we reran it on fresh examples. Two heavy tests at once both came out wrong. Small models tend to guess instead of saying "I don't know."

If this is you, do this: If you're about to pay for an AI tool, write the test first, use made-up data, run it twice and count a guess as a failure.

AI tools

Which local AI models fit in a 64 GB Mac mini?

What we checked
Apple's tech specs and the published download sizes of 4-bit model files.
The result
A 30B coding model (18.6 GB) and a 32B general model (19.8 GB) fit together, at about 38 GB. A 70B model (42.5 GB) fits, but leaves little room for long conversations.

If this is you, do this: If you want to run AI on your own machine, compare the model's file size to your memory and leave room for the system and long conversations.

How do you keep AI helpers from doing things they shouldn't?

What we checked
Field notes from running our own business with an AI crew.
The result
Our helpers draft and the owner decides. Every email, post, purchase and first contact waits for his yes. Limits that are built in work better than asking an AI nicely to behave.

If this is you, do this: If you hand work to helpers, AI or human, write down in one sentence what they can draft and what only you can send.
